# Revising a draft in Alex's voice

Use [the source excerpts](samples.md) and Alex's current brief together. The
excerpts demonstrate writing in context; they do not supply a formula for
sounding like him. For a channel without direct samples, follow the channel's
needs and avoid claiming an inferred preference is established evidence.

## Start with the account he wants to give

Identify what happened, why he cared, what he did, and what the reader needs to
understand. Preserve his chosen story. A technically interesting finding may
belong in a paragraph without becoming the thesis of the whole piece.

For the skills-from-traces post, Alex supplied the sequence: an expensive
refactor, analysis of Claude's LangWatch traces, changes to skills and agents,
comparison with fresh traces during use, then Scenario tests and saved model
responses. Replacing that account with a catalogue of research corrections lost
the purpose of the post.

Keep consequential qualifications with the claims they affect. A model-price
valuation is not evidence of a paid invoice, and comparing changing tasks does
not establish a percentage saving. Explain each limitation once, in proportion
to the claim. Do not turn the piece into an audit report.

## Revise the whole passage

Alex rejected both manufactured contrasts and a later draft full of staged
asides, tidy conclusions and tutorial sections. Changing a few banned words did
not address the structure that made those drafts sound generated.

Read adjacent paragraphs together. Follow the events and the reasoning without
stopping after every example to state its lesson. Remove sentences that merely
announce the significance of what the reader just read. Use headings where a
reader needs them, rather than dividing a personal account into instructions.

Keep the detail that explains the work. Allow sentence length and paragraph
length to follow the thought. A longer sentence can carry connected facts
naturally; splitting it into a setup, a punchline and a maxim can distort the
voice. Short sentences are equally fine when there is little to say.

Do not add humour, profanity, British colloquialisms, references, parenthetical
asides or self-deprecation to make a draft appear personal. Retain them when
Alex supplied them and they fit the new context. Do not invent experiences or
opinions to supply personality.

## Check before delivery

Read the complete draft against the brief and the source material. Check that
the facts support the account, the paragraphs connect, and the ending has
something left to say. An abrupt ending or an elaborate conclusion is not a
requirement of the voice.

Use British spelling, natural contractions and Alex's preference for spaced
hyphens or other punctuation instead of em dashes. Correct typing mistakes.

Phrase checks can catch a known rejected construction. They cannot determine
whether a post is interesting or sounds like Alex. Do not report a voice match
as established because automated checks pass.
